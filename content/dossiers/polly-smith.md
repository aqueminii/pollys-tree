Grand-matriarch of the lineage, Polly Smith emerged from chattel enslavement in Albemarle County to establish titled land ownership. Emancipated on March 23, 1865 (Albemarle Deed Book #61), she labored on Southall lands with her husband David while raising 12 verified children (Isaac, Elizabeth "Lizzie", Richard "Lot", Patsy/Patsey, Adaline/Addie, James, Charles "Charlie", Eliza, Mary, John, Albert, Harry Hudson) and accumulating capital.

In December 1889, she acquired an 18-acre commercial orchard and farmstead in Earlysville out of the Chancery suit *Brown's Administrator v. Burnley*. Holding the deed solely in her own name served as a defensive shield against post-emancipation vagrancy statutes, predatory tax defaults, and labor exploitation aimed at Black men.

In her final years, she relocated with her family to Hanover County, where cuttings and seeds from her Earlysville orchard were transplanted into Doswell soil.
