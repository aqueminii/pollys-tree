While Polly anchored the family home on her farm in Rivanna, her daughter Elizabeth "Lizzie" Smith provided the bridge to Charlottesville's urban core. Born April 1, 1855, Lizzie married Bigeton Newton on December 13, 1878. Bigeton acquired a home and lot at 303 North 3rd Street in Vinegar Hill—sitting directly on "Southall’s Meadow."

Lizzie gave birth to Hattie Belle Newton (June 1881) and Mattie Newton, but died of typhoid fever on November 27, 1881, at age 26. Polly stepped in, raising infant Hattie at her Rivanna farm. When Bigeton died in 1903 without a written will, the 303 N. 3rd Street house automatically fell under the control of the Charlottesville Corporation Court in the Chancery suit McDaniel v. Newton.

To understand why "fairness" was impossible, one must look at the judicial monopoly held by the Duke and Hanckel families. Judge R.T.W. Duke Sr. sat as Albemarle County Court Judge, while his son, R.T.W. Duke Jr., served as Charlottesville Corporation Court Judge and Commonwealth’s Attorney. At the exact same time they issued rulings from the bench, their private law firms (Duke & Duke and Hanckel & Hanckel) acted as court-appointed Special Commissioners. They weaponized minor debt claims (like undertaker bills) and missing heirs (Hattie's vanished brother John) to keep partition cases open for 30+ years, systematically draining Black family equity through recurring court fees.

Matching suitcase files with UVA records revealed a February 1933 letter from Lizzie's second daughter, Mattie Newton Jones, written from 207 East 98th Street in Harlem, asserting her direct inheritance rights alongside Hattie.
