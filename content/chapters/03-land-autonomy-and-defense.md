When legal pressure in Charlottesville reached a head, the family executed a strategic migration. Packing their hard-won equity, grand-matriarch Polly Smith relocated from her Rivanna farm to Hanover County near Doswell alongside Hattie, Alexander McDaniel, Sr., and their young children. Polly spent her final days on land her family owned outright.

Hattie assumed full management of the Hanover property. Annotating a 1906 auction broadside with legal definitions (decree = "order"; legal title = "safe/lawful right"), she wrote across court papers: "I will not be bluffed." She paid state poll taxes to preserve a paper trail of landownership and secured 47.5 acres of prime farmland in Doswell.

Hattie repeatedly confronted the system directly. When attorney Louis T. Hanckel, Jr. claimed in court filings that he held her brother John's missing-heir share in escrow, Hattie traveled from Doswell to Charlottesville to demand his portion. Hanckel dismissively turned her away, having absorbed John's share into court fees. Hattie returned home and preserved every receipt in Aunt Marilyn's brown leather suitcase.
